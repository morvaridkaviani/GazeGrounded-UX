## Study title

Where do people look when an interface makes a task hard?

## Researcher

[Name], [institution], [contact e-mail]. Supervisor: [name, e-mail] (if applicable).

## What is this study about?

We study how the design of websites affects how easily people complete simple tasks, using
your webcam to estimate where you look on the screen.

## What will I do?

About 15 minutes: a short eye-tracking calibration (clicking on dots), then about 9 tasks on
pictures of websites (for example, “Change the delivery address”), each followed by one rating
and an optional comment.

## How is my webcam used?

* An open-source program (WebGazer.js) running **inside your browser** estimates where on the
  screen you are looking.
* **No video or photos of you are recorded, saved or sent anywhere.**
* Only the estimated screen coordinates, your clicks, timings and your answers are kept.

## What data is collected?

| Collected | Not collected |
|---|---|
| A participant code given to you by the researcher | Your name, e-mail or IP address (we do not log them) |
| Estimated gaze coordinates on the task images | Webcam video or images |
| Clicks, task times, success | Your browser history or other tabs |
| Ease ratings and comments you write | Any data from other applications |
| Screen size and calibration quality | |

Please do not write personal information in the comment fields.

## Is gaze data sensitive?

Gaze patterns are behavioural data. In large amounts they can reveal things about attention,
reading, fatigue or potentially health. We collect a few minutes on prepared images only, store
it under a code, and use it solely for this research.

## What happens to my data?

* It stays in your browser until **you** choose to download it and send it to the researcher
  at the end of the study.
* It is stored on [secure storage], accessible to [the research team only], kept for
  [retention period], then deleted.
* Results are published only in aggregate. Anonymised data may be shared with other
  researchers [only if you agree below].

## Voluntary participation and withdrawal

Taking part is voluntary. You can stop at any time by closing the browser tab; nothing is kept.
After sending your data, you can ask for it to be deleted until [date] by e-mailing the
researcher with your participant code, without giving a reason.

## Risks and benefits

No known risks beyond normal screen use. There is no direct benefit; you help improve how
